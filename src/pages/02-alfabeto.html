26 letters, same as English

Since the 2009 spelling reform, k, w and y officially belong to the Portuguese alphabet, so you have the same 26 letters as in English. ç and the accents are not extra letters: they change how a letter sounds.

1. As letras · The letters

Click a letter to hear its name and an example word. Use 🐢 for slow.

🇩🇪 Letter names are different from German

E is é (not “eh”), G is gê (like German “je”), H is agá, J is jota, W is dáblio, V is vê and X is xis. Letters ending in -e, like éfe, éle, ême, end with a soft “i” sound in Brazil.

Marc → “Márki”

Portuguese words rarely end in a consonant sound, so Brazilians add a little “i”: Marc becomes “Márki”. The same happens with Facebook → “Feicibúqui” and hip-hop → “ripóqui”. Do not be surprised when you hear it!

4. O alfabeto de soletração · The spelling alphabet

On the phone Brazilians spell with words: “M de mala, A de avião, R de rato, C de casa.” There is no single official list, but these words are common and clear.

Para a professora · For the teacher

Faça o Marc soletrar em voz alta: nome, sobrenome, e-mail (“arroba”, “ponto”, “ponto com”), endereço. Em formulários no Brasil pedem “Como se escreve?” o tempo todo. Observe: R (érre) e H (agá) são as mais difíceis para alemães.

Have Marc spell out loud: first name, surname, e-mail (“arroba” = @, “ponto” = dot, “ponto com” = .com), address. In Brazil people constantly ask “Como se escreve?”. Note: R (érre) and H (agá) are the hardest for Germans.
