How to read the symbols

The IPA (International Phonetic Alphabet) writes one symbol per sound, between square brackets: [ˈka.zɐ] is casa. The mark ˈ comes before the stressed syllable. You do not need to memorize all of it; use it as a map to check how a word really sounds. Every card below plays an example.

1. Vogais orais · Oral vowels

Brazilian Portuguese has seven stressed oral vowels. Click each one.

🇩🇪 You already have most of these sounds

Brazilian [ɛ] = German “ä” in “Bär” · [e] = “ee” in “Tee” · [ɔ] = “o” in “Gott” · [o] = “oo” in “Boot” · [u] = “u” in “gut”. The main difference: Portuguese vowels do not get “long vs short” treatment, and unstressed vowels stay clear, not swallowed.

2. O “e” e o “o” no fim da palavra · The “e” and “o” at the end of a word

This is the most noticeable Brazilian feature: unstressed final vowels are different from the spelling.

Common mistake for German speakers

German speakers often say a final -e like a schwa (as in “Blume”). In Brazil it is “i”: leite = “lêitchi”, grande = “grândji”, noite = “nôitchi”. Final -o sounds like “u”: livro = “lívru”.

3. Vogais nasais · Nasal vowels

Air goes through the nose. Do not pronounce an extra “m” or “n”: the vowel itself is nasal, like in the German word “Restaurant” or “Balkon”.

4. Ditongos · Diphthongs

🇩🇪 Not the same as German

Portuguese ei = [ej] (like “ay” in “day”), not German “ei” [aɪ] · eu = [ew], not German “eu” [ɔʏ] · au = [aw] like German “au”.

7. Qual é a sílaba tônica? · Which syllable is stressed?

The stressed syllable is the strongest. Click it. Rule of thumb: words ending in a, e, o, as, es, os, am, em are stressed on the second-to-last syllable; words ending in i, u, l, r, z or nasals (ão, im, um) on the last. An accent mark always wins.

9. Teste de ouvido · Ear test

Para a professora · For the teacher

Ordem sugerida: (1) t/d + i (tchi, dji), (2) -e e -o finais, (3) nasais (pão, mãe, bom), (4) R forte: o alemão pode usar o R uvular dele, que funciona bem no início de palavra e em “rr”, (5) L final = u. Peça repetição em coro curto e depois frases completas. Use espelho ou vídeo para as nasais. Mantenha o alfabeto fonético como consulta, não como prova.

Suggested order: (1) t/d + i (tchi, dji), (2) final -e and -o, (3) nasals (pão, mãe, bom), (4) strong R: the German speaker can use his uvular R, which works well at the start of words and in “rr”, (5) final L = u. Ask for short choral repetition first, then full sentences. Use a mirror or video for nasals. Keep the phonetic alphabet as a reference, not as a test.
